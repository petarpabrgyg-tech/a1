How should fine cambric dress shirts be laundered?

We advise washing by hand or in a gentle cycle with lukewarm water (30°C) using mild organic soap flakes. Never subject cambric shirts to commercial industrial laundry presses, which crack natural mother-of-pearl buttons and crush floating collar interlinings. Line dry on shaped wooden hangers away from direct baking sunlight.

What is the secret to pressing and starching a cambric collar?

Iron the shirt while the cambric remains slightly damp from laundering. Apply a natural rice-water or arrowroot starch mist to the collar and cuffs, pressing with a medium-hot iron from the outer collar points inward toward the center neckband to prevent unsightly fabric ripples along the point edges.

How many fittings are required for a first-time bespoke order?

First-time commissions require two consultations: an initial anatomical measurement and fabric selection session, followed by a preliminary cotton muslin toile fitting to verify neckband slope, armhole pitch, and cuff drop before cutting the final cambric cloth.

What distinguishes genuine batiste de Cambrai from ordinary lawn or poplin?

Poplin features a pronounced horizontal rib created by heavier weft yarns, giving it a stiffer, denser drape. Lawn uses slightly less twisted yarns for an open, airy feel. Cambric utilizes balanced, tightly twisted fine yarns calendared under heavy cylinders to achieve a smooth, calendared surface sheen with exceptional tensile snap.

Can historical ruff collars or Victorian chemises be commissioned?

Yes. Our costume master and sartorial historians frequently construct period-accurate Elizabethan pleated ruffs, poet chemises, and Napoleonic cravats for theater productions, opera houses, and private sartorial collectors worldwide.
